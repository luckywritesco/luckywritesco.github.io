PROLOGUE

Purpose

An illustration by Lucky David

A school is like a child’s name: it carries meaning and guides that child’s growth. They say a good name is far more precious than silver and gold. We live our lives with a purpose we hope to fulfil, and discovering it helps us along the journey. Here is a little about me and my purpose-driven life.

I love writing, but I allowed people’s opinions and validation to get to me. I was discouraged at some point, and I forgot what it meant to write.

In secondary school, I was in a science class and deeply admired Ben Carson through his books. He went from not knowing to being the intelligent man in the room, and he applied it in a profession recognised as one of the most renowned in the world. He impacted humanity. He found his path.

As a young girl, there was nothing I could think of in the world apart from being a doctor, although everyone who knew me well could tell I was just beating drums. Maybe I didn’t believe hard enough. I felt that being a science student was not my calling, because I was an extrovert, the “life of the party” growing up, and I assumed science students were supposed to be calm, collected and soaked in books. But that did not matter to me, because my father was very sick, and all I could think of was saving his life and giving him proper treatment. I was only a teenager then, and all of it came from the love I had for my father.

In SS 2, I lost my father. I was in boarding school, where students collect an exit form whenever they are going home, and I had never collected one before. Then my guardian in school told me that my father wanted me to accompany him to the hospital for dialysis, and that the form was already collected. I was writing exams, but for some reason, they let me go. I was so excited about my first exit form that I sensed nothing.

The dialysis was not new to me. In the break before the exams, I had begged to go with him to the hospital. I was late packing, so he left without me, and my mother gave me transport money to follow him. There I watched his blood pass through a machine and back into his body. He told me the machine was cleaning his blood. That was the last time I saw my father alive. Looking back, that moment means a lot to me. I know now that the dialysis was only the reason they gave me to bring me home.

On the way, my older brother, who had already taken down the posters around the area, stopped the rickshaw at a corner near our house. I jumped up in excitement when I saw him. He held me by the neck and made me promise that whatever I saw, I would not be sad and I would not cry. I did not understand, but my heart was already racing. I could hear the mourning song, “It Won’t Be Long, We Will Be Going Home.” When I reached our gate, I saw my father’s poster: “Gone Too Soon.”

I collapsed. They carried me in, still in my school uniform.

We travelled to the village for the burial, after first going to the mortuary to pick up his body. The loud cry from everyone there was the first time it began to feel real, and even then, it did not fully hit me until he was laid in the ground. I did not stay up to three days, because I still had exams to write, and I had to rush back to school.

My father died before I could complete secondary school, go to university and move on to medical school. I was heartbroken, and I felt my dream of medical school had shattered. I tried to move to the art class because I no longer felt the point of staying in science, but my brothers and sisters strongly opposed it. They did not change how I felt, but I stayed.

I was not staying out of fear of them, but out of reason. I realised that doctors are not just for a single patient, and that the world needs me. So I pushed on.

In Nigeria, when a sibling dies, it is normal for family members to take in the children, one or two, depending on their capacity. In the second term of SS 2, my aunty took me in, and by SS 3, I was already living with her and her family.

After that, I wrote JAMB and applied to UI (University of Ibadan) to study Medicine and Surgery, but my JAMB score was below the cut-off point, so there was no way I would be admitted.

I did not give up on medicine, because it was the course I had applied for. But Taraba State University, where the cut-off point matched my score, was fairly new and did not offer Medicine and Surgery. So I changed institutions and chose Computer Science, the only reasonable course I could apply for at the time. The same year, before the admission list came out, I rewrote JAMB in case I was not admitted. Fortunately, after much struggle, I got admission with my initial score, and I was very excited to be settling into a degree programme.

When I got to 300 level and we were sent out for IT, I chose a placement that was more audible, with more reading, less calculating, fewer codes and more speaking, hoping to work in a fabulous and well-known network or television station. So I went for Networking and System Security. I got excited just from the name and started to imagine big companies and a good life.

When I got to 400 level, the story changed. I fell in love, and that marked the beginning of a milestone and, in time, the birth of VH Lines Tech.

CHAPTER 1

The First Day

The way he tells it

I met my husband in 200 level, in a Computer Science class. For me, it was the course I settled for after my first choice, Medicine and Surgery at the University of Ibadan, did not work out. For him, it was a passion for programming.

He had come in as a Direct Entry (DE) student. He already had a diploma, so he started from 200 level, while I started from 100 level as a fresh JAMB applicant. By the time he walked into our class, I had already spent a year there.

This first day is really his story, because he is the one who tells it.

In those days, I lived far from school, so catching early lessons was almost impossible. Most of the time, I came in late, so I always sat at the far end of the class and slipped in through the back door.

This is how he describes what he saw that day: a girl in a blue and white striped polo T-shirt and jeans, wearing her medicated glasses and looking mean. 😂

And yet, he says, his heart skipped a beat. In his mind, he thought, “Who is this fine girl?”

I, on the other hand, had no idea. I was only trying to find a seat.

P.S. What are the chances? A class of over 100 coursemates, a girl slipping in late through the back door, and a boy whose heart skipped a beat. 🫢❤️

CHAPTER 2

The First Time I Spoke to My Husband

The first time I ever spoke to my husband at university was during Mr. Andebutop’s test.

The test was on a Saturday morning, and because I lived quite far from school, I took a rickshaw straight to campus so I wouldn’t miss it.

On the way in, we passed my husband, who was not even a friend then. In my mind, I thought, “I know this guy. Should we stop and give him a ride?” Before I could decide, we had already driven past.

When I got to class, the test had already started. It was a theory test, so I needed a fullscape sheet. I rushed to the shops around the cafeteria, but most of them were closed, and the only one that was open didn’t sell fullscape sheets.

Panicking, I hurried back toward the classroom. My plan was to beg the lecturer to let one of my coursemates come out and tear a sheet for me.

Then, halfway to class, I saw him.

He was standing there with his 80-leaf long notebook, tearing out a sheet.

I literally ran toward him and pleaded, “Please, can I have a sheet?”

Without saying a single word, he handed it to me. I grabbed it, thanked him in a rush, and ran back into class to write my test.

After the test, as usual, everyone gathered in small groups to discuss their answers. He was standing with his friends, so I walked up to him and said, “Thank you for the fullscape sheet.”

He heard me… but he neither turned nor said a word. 😩😂 So I quietly walked away with my friend.

I found out later that the moment I left, one of his friends nudged him and said,

“See, she just spoke to you! Go and shoot your shot!”

Shoot your shot? Why? (Chapter 4 explains. 😂)

P.S. We started as strangers, became friends, then lovers, and now we are soulmates and partners in everything. If I could live our story again, I wouldn’t change a single thing. God designed it that way, so that we would learn, be grateful, and give Him glory.

P.S. 2: Our love story is my favourite love story.

CHAPTER 3

Mr. Dell

How I got my husband’s attention back in uni

We were given a programming assignment, and by the next day, my husband had already solved it.

Many of our classmates were making photocopies of his solution, so all I kept hearing was:

“Who solved the assignment?”
“It’s AB! It’s AB!”

At that point, I was wondering, “Who is this AB?” He had come in as a Direct Entry student, so I had not caught his name yet.

When I finally found out who AB was, I remembered we had actually crossed paths before. (You already know that story. 😂)

I have always liked intelligent people, so I naturally gravitate towards them. I wanted to start a conversation with him, but he was busy tutoring some of our coursemates, so I waited patiently.

When they were done and he was packing up, I called out, “Mr. Dell!”

The reason? The laptop he was using at the time was a Dell.

He looked up with a smile, almost laughing, as if he was genuinely excited that I had spoken to him. 😆

“My name is Abraham, but you can call me AB,” he said.

Now, I have a personal rule: I don’t call people by their nicknames… because why would I? 😂 We both laughed, and I told him I would rather call him Mr. Dell.

P.S. How would you feel if your love at first sight started a real conversation with you?

P.S. 2: I’m sure every time he opens his laptop and sees the Dell logo, he remembers the person who calls him Mr. Dell. 😆

CHAPTER 4

How My Husband Shot His Shot

We had been friends since 2016, and somewhere along the way I realised my husband liked me. But he waited until our final year to say anything.

The only question on my mind was… why isn’t this guy saying anything? 😂

Apart from being a computer scientist, I also doubled as a motivational speaker and relationship coach. 😆

One day, we went to our favourite reading spot to study. After a while, we took a break, and somehow the conversation shifted to relationships.

I said, “I don’t understand why someone would like a person and still not tell them.”

One of our coursemates replied, “What if I tell her I like her, and she doesn’t feel the same way? What if it ruins our friendship?”

I went into full counselling mode.

“At least you’ve put it out there,” I said. “Go ahead and ask her, even now.”

I kept talking, giving all the relationship advice I could think of. 😂 Plenty gist, sha…

Then, at the end of our conversation, my husband said, “Lucky… I like you.”

I giggled. 🤭

“Don’t tell me,” I said. “Go and tell the person you like.” (Pretending as if I didn’t know. 😅)

He looked at me again and said, “You’re the one I like.”

At that point, all we could do was laugh.

We eventually went home, but that conversation marked the beginning of our love story. And that was how our ship began to sail. ❤️

P.S. This is your sign to encourage that man to do the right thing. 😂 Sometimes, all he needs is a little push.

CHAPTER 5

History Has It on Us

Looking back, I realise that my husband and I have shared a lot of memories, some intentional, and some that only make sense now.

Our first job interview was together. We were discharged from NYSC in March 2021, and I went back to Abuja, where I was based at the time. In June, I came down to Taraba for my friend’s wedding, which was around the same time Stanbic IBTC was recruiting. We were invited for an interview, so I went with my then-boyfriend, now my husband, and some of our coursemates. I had always wanted a banking job, but God had other plans.

Looking back now, I find it interesting how our journeys have always somehow intersected.

Even my first business, Data SME, was influenced by my husband back in university. He built a website where people could buy and transact data, and somehow I found myself part of that journey too.

I have always been more of a corporate-job kind of person. 😂 My husband, on the other hand, prefers remote or hybrid work.

But look at us now… somehow, we have both found ourselves navigating corporate, remote and hybrid work. 😂

And then came VH Lines Tech.

What started as an idea is slowly becoming a dream we are watching come to life. A dream born from our love for technology, teaching, communication, and bringing imaginations to life.

Communication between God and man.
Man and man.
And man and his computer. 😂

Looking back, I realise our story has never been only about falling in love and getting married.

We’ve built.
We’ve learned.
We’ve tried.
We’ve failed.
We’ve started again.

Through all the different seasons, we have kept finding ourselves on the same journey. Maybe that is one of the beautiful things about finding your person: not that you will always share the same dreams or preferences, but that you can grow, evolve and build something meaningful together.

And this chapter?

VH Lines Tech is a dream we are still actualising. ❤️

EPILOGUE

Purpose, Revisited

If you had told that girl who wanted to save her father’s life that she would one day be building a tech company with a man who once handed her a sheet of paper without saying a word, she would have said, “Me? In computers?” 😂

But I understand it now. I wanted to save one life, and purpose had a bigger plan.

My purpose is aligned with my husband’s. Together, we are building VH Lines Tech into a conglomerate, raising a godly family, making an impact in society and spreading love.

And the girl who forgot what it meant to write? She is writing again. This series is the proof.

The best, I believe, is yet to come.
