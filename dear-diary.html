Dear Diary

A personal essay by Lucky David

My name is Lucky David. I don’t remember much of my childhood, but I know one thing: I love writing.

It started very simply, with a paper diary. Every tear, every moment, every pain, and every happiness was jotted down on a sheet of paper. I also love reading, almost anything I see. Sometimes I feel my life is a history that should be told, so I kept my memories, the good, sad, and sour ones, and hid them from people.

Once in a while, I go back through the old pages to see the dates and times. Every time, I feel something undefined, towards a memory and towards how I handled myself.

Why the hell do I need diaries to remind me of my past? I call it the unforgiven past, and it has influenced so many things in my life. All I remember is how I was an emotional fool, instead of being a woman who lets nothing get to her. All I remember is how I underestimated myself and let people walk over me.

One day, I was flipping through my diary, and I reached the pages about my first love. I read all my write-ups and expressions of feeling and could not believe I had ever been that romantic:

“My heart yearns for you.
You are the only man I see when my eyes are closed.
I know I’m captivated by you.
My life is meaningless without you.
You brought the sun into my life and made it full of sunshine.
I will stand by you no matter what the situation might be, my sunshine.”

That is history now, more than a decade old. Reading it has its benefits. I laugh at some of my foolish actions and expressions. I cry at some of them. And some remind me of what I would rather not remember.

For a while, I was confused. I thought keeping a diary might be good for happy people, but not for me. Okay, let me not sound pessimistic. A diary is where the untold stories and fears are written just for the diary and the “diaryer.” No third party.

But I have found peace with my diaries. When I read my old thoughts, I see how strong my emotions can be, and I see how much my writing has changed as I have. It feels good now to read them and laugh at my own feelings, experiences, and thoughts.

The girl in those pages was not a fool. She was just writing her way towards me.
