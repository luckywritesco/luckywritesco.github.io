Delays Are Not Denials

My husband liked me from our 200 level, but he didn’t ask me out until our 400 level.

Who knows? Maybe if he had asked me out earlier, we wouldn’t be here today as husband and wife.

Today, I want to talk about delays.

Delay simply means having to wait longer than expected. Most of us don’t like waiting, because we naturally want things to happen quickly. We often see delays as something negative, but what if a delay is actually for our learning, our growth, our gratitude and, ultimately, God’s glory?

💡 Jesus didn’t begin His earthly ministry until He was about 30 years old.

💡 God promised Abraham a son, but Isaac didn’t come until Abraham was 100 years old. God also promised that he would become the father of many nations. That promise wasn’t fulfilled overnight; it unfolded through generations, and today we are still singing the children’s song about Father Abraham and his many sons.

💡 Joseph dreamed that he would become a great man. But before the palace came betrayal, slavery, false accusations and prison. There was a process before the promise.

💡 Remember David and the cave of Adullam? It is my favourite story. An anointed king had to wait for years to sit on the throne, and he fought many battles along the way.

The Bible is filled with stories of people whose purpose was preceded by waiting.

In our own story, God allowed us to go through a process for our growth, for us to appreciate the journey, and for His glory.

Sometimes, delays are God’s way of protecting us.

Protecting us from wrong decisions.
Protecting us from lust.
Protecting us from compromise.
Protecting us from unhealthy relationships.
Or simply preparing us for what we’ve been praying for.

So, start looking at delays from a different perspective.

Not every delay is a setback. Sometimes, it’s God’s way of saying, “Not yet, because I’m working on something better.”

Remember, delays are not denials.

Stay blessed.

Marry Your Friend

I don’t subscribe to marrying your best friend.

Relationships are too serious to be playing around as friends.

Well, that was what I thought when I started dating.

Was it my upbringing or my environment? I think it was both. I thought friendship and relationships were two different things. I didn’t understand why people said, “Marry your friend.”

Today, I say to you:

Marry your friend!!

Because when the tides get rough, friendship is one of the things that can keep you anchored.

When the butterflies settle.
When the excitement of being newly married becomes normal life.
When bills and responsibilities come.
When you disagree.
When you are not particularly happy with each other...

Friendship matters.

I married my friend. And I can tell you, it makes a difference.

Conversations are easy.
Laughter comes naturally.
I can be silly around him.
I can be vulnerable around him.

And my heart is at ease, because I know his heart.

Before he became my husband, he became someone I genuinely enjoyed being around.

And today, I don’t just love my husband.

I like him.
I enjoy him.
I can gist with him.
I can annoy him. 😂

And after everything... I know that’s my person.

So, yes... I changed my mind.

Marry your friend o!!!

He Knows My Heart

He gets jealous
when I hold his pillows and neglect him. 😂

He remembers the things I used to say to other people
and somehow finds a way to use my own words to address me.

He knows my heart,
sometimes even before I have the courage to let it out.

He chooses to be reserved, plain and simple.
I can’t be like that,
but somehow, loving him makes me want to slow down, listen and understand.

He has this quiet way of saying,
“I will always be here.”
And somehow, it makes tomorrow feel less frightening.

He taught me to stop living in yesterday,
to stop carrying the weight of everything I thought I had lost.

Because the truth is,
I wasn’t always the easiest person to love.

With him... it was different.
He wasn’t just in my life.
He was in every word I wrote...

And perhaps only a fool would reject the nourishment sitting right in front of them and choose to starve instead.

I looked at him.
And every time he smiled at me, I saw pieces of myself I had forgotten:

the joy I had denied myself,
the love I was afraid to receive,
the tenderness I didn’t know how to accept.

And I found myself saying,
“As you learn to love yourself better, teach me.
As you learn to forgive yourself for failing, teach me.
As you learn to embrace your strength, teach me.”

Sometimes, I wonder if I ever truly knew what love was before him.
Or maybe... I never found true love until now.

I love you, Abraham Idris.

Before the World Knows

Before anyone knows, my husband knows first. My very first and only confidant.

He is the very first to know about my plans, my problems, my debts 😂 and my wins.

Before I make any decision, I want my husband to know first and to consent to it.

Yes, I need to tell my husband.
Yes, I need to ask my husband first.
That has always been my first thought.

Because if I fall, he carries me. If I win, he applauds me. If I fail, he comforts me.

Before anyone else, he is there.

But this isn’t one-sided.

My husband was once about to make a very important decision. He could have consulted anyone else, but he called me.

He explained the situation and asked me what I thought. I listened, considered everything, and eventually told him what I believed was the best decision.

Then he said something that stayed with me: “I’m taking this with your consent.”

That struck me.

My counsel was appreciated. My opinion mattered. I felt important and valued, knowing that my voice had a place in the decisions that affected our lives. And I remember feeling so glad that I was building my life with this man.

It’s knowing that your partner isn’t just beside you...
They are with you.

And that’s one of the things I cherish most about us.

Before the world knows...
My husband knows first. ❤️
